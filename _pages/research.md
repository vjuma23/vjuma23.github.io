My research focuses on mathematical and computational approaches to biological systems, particularly problems in which nonlinear interactions generate complex temporal or spatial behaviour.

## Reaction–diffusion systems and pattern formation

I study diffusion-driven instability, Turing pattern formation, oscillatory reaction–diffusion systems, and Floquet theory for spatial instabilities around time-periodic states. This work combines linear stability analysis, bifurcation theory, asymptotic reasoning, and numerical simulation.

## Cell signalling and Rho GTPase dynamics

A major strand of my work concerns mathematical models of Rho GTPase signalling and Rho–myosin regulation. I develop and analyse activator–inhibitor and mechanochemical models to understand oscillations, bistability, cell polarization, contraction, and spatial patterning.

## Mechanochemical modelling

I am developing mechanochemical models of capsular contracture, coupling cell recruitment, collagen dynamics, active traction, viscoelastic stress, and tissue deformation. The work includes one-dimensional analysis and finite-element simulations in annular and higher-dimensional geometries.

## Cancer and radiotherapy modelling

My work in cancer modelling includes tumour growth, heterogeneous tissue properties, radiative particle transport, dose distributions, and the response of tumour populations to fractionated radiotherapy.

## Epidemiological modelling

I have worked on mathematical models of infectious-disease transmission, with particular emphasis on COVID-19 in Kenya. These studies use compartmental models, parameter estimation, intervention scenarios, vaccination, and multi-wave epidemic dynamics.

## Numerical and computational methods

My computational work uses finite-element methods, ODE/PDE solvers, numerical continuation, parameter estimation, and scientific computing. I work primarily with FEniCS/FEniCSx, PETSc, MATLAB, Python, Gmsh, and visualization tools such as ParaView and PyVista.
